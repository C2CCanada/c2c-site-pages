The demand the housing models aren’t tracking.

Two federal commitments are converging on CFB Esquimalt. None of that demand shows up in the current housing models. Our buildings are already in its path.

By Colin Ernst · Co-Founder June 4, 2026

Our three Victoria projects continue to move along their own timelines. Emerson is selling slowly in a soft Victoria core market, and we are lining up JV partners to hold a couple of units long-term. Craigflower’s roof is going on, with a revised completion target of November 30. Wilson-Hereward is funded at Coast Capital, with demolition recently underway and pre-fabricated wall panels in the build plan.

The operational updates matter. But the story worth telling this month is the ground all three projects are standing on.

Two federal commitments, one neighbourhood

Craigflower and Wilson-Hereward both sit within a kilometre of CFB Esquimalt, the Pacific home of the Royal Canadian Navy. Two federal commitments are converging on that base simultaneously.

The first is Canada’s Indo-Pacific posture. More naval presence in the western Pacific runs through Esquimalt. Personnel rotate in, contractors deploy, and the operational tempo of the base increases. All of that drives housing demand in the surrounding neighbourhood.

The second is Canada’s commitment to hit NATO’s 2% defence-spending target. That target translates into fleet renewal, the next-generation submarine program, and a wave of civilian contractors and posted personnel who have to live somewhere when they are assigned to Victoria.

“None of that demand shows up in the current housing models, and none of it goes away with election cycles. It is a current we cannot engineer. Our two newest buildings happen to sit directly in its path.”

Colin Ernst, Co-Founder

Why the models miss it

Standard housing demand models track population growth, income levels, and resale market signals. They do not model defence procurement cycles or Indo-Pacific force posture decisions. That is not a flaw in the models. It is simply outside the scope of what residential market analysis is built to capture.

The result is a demand driver that is real, durable, and largely invisible to conventional analysis. New navy families need housing close to the base. Civilian contractors working on fleet renewal need housing close to the base. That demand is not speculative. It follows from budget commitments already made and force posture decisions already announced.

What this means for the portfolio

We did not build near Esquimalt because we ran a projection model. We built there because we understand the neighbourhood, the demand drivers, and the kind of tenants who want to live in well-managed housing close to where they work. The federal tailwinds are a confirmation of a thesis, not the origin of it.

This is the part of real estate that rewards operators who are paying attention to more than the listings page. The returns come from understanding what is happening before it shows up in the data.
